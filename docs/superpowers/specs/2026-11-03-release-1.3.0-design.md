## Part A: engine

### #42 A command is carried out at the moment it was given

#### Why

A command is carried out when it gets the timer's lock, not when it was given. Usually the gap
is milliseconds. It reaches about 30 seconds while a slow device or a slow Workday sensor holds
the lock. "Add time" given just before the end then counts as a new run, and `started_at`
restarts. While Home Assistant starts, an item that falls due during the wait is folded before
the command and loses to it, when it should have come after it and won.

The research measured a worse case. The device usually reports while the slow call still holds
the lock. Its report then folds the end first, with its own later moment. The device is turned
off and on again: a flap.

#### The rule

- A command takes its moment when it is given, before it waits for the lock. The outcome is
  the one it would have had at that moment.
- "Add time" given before the end extends the countdown, even when the end passed while the
  command waited. There is one "Countdown started", no "Countdown ended", and `started_at` is
  kept. The device is not switched off and on.
- Taking time off that ends the countdown, given before the end, ends it as `stopped`, not
  `expired`. The device is turned off once.
- "Run for 10" ends 10 minutes after it was given, not 10 minutes after the lock was free.
- A delay counts from the moment the command was given. A delay that passed while the command
  waited is carried out at once. A moment (`at`) that was ahead when the command was given is
  accepted, even when it passed during the wait. A moment already past when it was given is
  still refused with `command_in_past`.
- While Home Assistant starts, an item that falls due after the moment given is folded after
  the command, so it wins.
- Commands given one after another are carried out in that order.
- A device's report and an alarm take their moment when they arrive, by the same rule.

#### Claude's calls

- **Reports and alarms are stamped too.** Stamping only the commands does not fix the common
  case (the research's table: the flap stays). So `_command`, `_handle_state_change` and
  `_handle_wakeup` each read the clock as their first line. The moment is the real clock, not
  `event.time_fired`, which can be earlier than a command queued before it. One loop thread and
  a first-in, first-out lock keep the moments in queue order.
- **The catch-up folds to the moment given.** The windows are worked out for that moment and
  folded to it, and the refresh after a window ends uses it too. An item due between that
  moment and the real time stays armed. Its alarm, armed in the past, fires on the next loop
  turn and folds it after the command.
- **The delay rule flips on purpose.** Today a delay counts "from the moment the command gets
  its turn", and one test pins it. 1.3.0 counts it from the moment given, because that is what
  the user did. The test is rewritten for the new rule, and so are the docstrings of
  `async_add_command` and `async_turn_off_in`.
- **Not stamped: start-up, window refreshes and the interval and schedule switches.** They learn
  windows from the Workday sensor and must fold to the time it answered. Stamping the switch
  would carry a command out before the window it follows (pinned by
  `test_window_made_by_a_slow_refresh_comes_before_a_later_command`).
- **A race stays, and is written down.** A refresh that waited for a slow sensor can fold an end
  that passed meanwhile before a command given earlier and queued behind it. It is the same as
  today and is not a defect of this fix.
- **The core does not change its signatures.** Every core method already takes `now`. The guard
  in `add` that treats a passed end as no end stays, because a late alarm can still bring one.
  The three comments that cite #42 are reworded.
- **Rejected:** a `tick` to the moment given before every command (a passed end would send a
  turn-off and then a turn-on); a clamp so time never goes back (a real clock step back would
  freeze the core); applying commands outside the lock (the lock also orders the awaits of the
  calls to the device).

#### Non-goals

- A change to what a command does once it runs. Only its moment changes.
- The residual race of a slow window refresh above.

#### Scope

`custom_components/timerify/controller.py` (the command path, the report and alarm handlers,
the catch-up and the refresh helpers, with an optional moment that keeps today's clock reading
for the other callers). Comments in `core.py`.

#### Docs

`docs/en/services.md` and `docs/ru/services.md`, "Calling the actions": a call that waits behind
a slow device is carried out as at the moment it was made, and its delay counts from then.
`pending-commands.md` in both languages, "How a pending command behaves", gets the same sentence
for a delay. Both languages in one commit.

#### Tests

Each with frozen time and a held call (`hold_next_call()` on the fake device, climate or Workday
sensor), at most 25 seconds per held call, and no `asyncio.wait_for`.

1. Add time behind a slow call, when the end passes during the wait: the end is the old end
   plus the time added, `started_at` is kept, one start, no end, no turn-off. The same with no
   slow call gives the same outcome.
2. The same with the device's report queued ahead of the command: no turn-off and turn-on.
   This test fails if only the commands are stamped.
3. Taking time off, given before the end and run after it: the countdown ends as `stopped`, and
   the device is turned off once.
4. "Run for 10" behind a slow call: "Ends at" is the press plus 10 minutes.
5. "Turn off in 1 minute" behind 75 seconds of slow calls: turned off at once, "Pending command
   came due" fired, "Next command" empty. A delay of 5 minutes behind the same wait: pending at
   the moment given plus 5 minutes.
6. A moment ahead when asked and past when the lock frees: accepted and carried out. A moment
   already past when asked: refused with `command_in_past`.
7. While starting: a stored "off" due 20 seconds in, a Workday interval with the sensor held,
   "Run for 10" given 10 seconds in. The stored "off" folds after the command, and the device is
   off. Without the held sensor nothing changes.
8. Add time then Stop, queued in that order behind a slow call: the timer ends stopped.
9. A command that waited and found the timer unloaded still fails with `timer_unloaded`.

### #47 Events carry the context of what caused them

#### Why

`timerify_countdown_started`, `timerify_countdown_ended` and `timerify_command_due` are fired
with a fresh context. The trace and the logbook of an automation that a Timerify trigger runs
then do not say what caused it: an action, a button, a voice command or a switch of the device.

#### The rule

- An event caused by a person or an automation carries the context of that cause. That covers
  the actions, the Run for entity, the Turn off button, an interval switch, the card (its calls
  are the same actions), Assist sentences, the `timerify__` tools of an LLM agent, and the
  device switched on or off by hand.
- The logbook of an automation run by such an event reads "triggered by action timerify.run",
  with the user who called it. Its trace links to the call.
- An event caused by time gets a fresh context, with no user and no parent. That covers a
  countdown that ran out, a limit that came, a pending command that came due and the countdown
  it starts, a schedule window that starts or ends, and the catch-up at start-up.
- Which events fire, and in which order, does not change.

#### Claude's calls

- **The same context, not a child.** The cause's `Context` object itself is passed to
  `hass.bus.async_fire`. Home Assistant's convention is that a state or an event caused by a
  call carries the call's context unchanged. The logbook looks only one parent up, so a child
  would lose the user.
- **Carried per call, never read at fire time.** The controller's public methods take
  `context: Context | None = None`, and it travels with the change to `_announce`. An entity's
  `_context` is not read when the event fires: it can be stale by then (Home Assistant clears it
  only at a later state write).
- **Time-caused announcements are always fresh, whatever the batch.** "Add time" on an end that
  has passed announces `expired` and a new start in one batch. Only the start is the user's
  doing, so `expired`, `limit` and `CommandCameDue` get a fresh context and the rest carry the
  batch's.
- **Switching an interval on inside its open window.** The countdown that starts carries the
  switch's context: the person caused it, though the window's end sets its length. The window
  is folded in the same batch (`_async_refresh_windows_locked`, then `_fold_due`), so the
  context is passed through those helpers too, not stopped at `_execute_fold`. A window that
  opens later by itself still gets a fresh context. (Settled after the builder check.)
- **A known imprecision, written in the docstring.** A device's report whose catch-up also folds
  a due window or command carries the report's context on that start.
- **A pending command is not linked to the call that made it.** That would store the context id
  in the timer's file (a schema change) for no clear gain.
- **Out of scope:** calling the device with a child context, so the logbook would say the device
  was switched "by Timerify, after the user". One line in `followups.md` if the owner wants it.

#### Non-goals

- A new event, a new trigger or new event data.
- Context for the websocket command `timerify/timer`, which only reads.

#### Scope

`controller.py` (the nine public methods, `_command`, `_execute`, `_execute_fold`, `_announce`,
`_handle_state_change` with `event.context`). The callers: `number.py`, `button.py` and
`switch.py` pass `self._context`; the voice handlers pass `intent_obj.context`. `trigger.py`
already passes `event.context` on and does not change.

#### Docs

`docs/en/automations.md` and `docs/ru/automations.md`, "What a trigger tells the automation":
the trace and the logbook name the cause and the user, and an event caused by time names none.
`services.md`, "Events", in both languages: one sentence on the context. One commit.

#### Tests

Events captured with `async_capture_events`, with a real user (`hass_admin_user`).

1. For each way in (`timerify.run`, `add_time`, `stop`, `number.set_value`, the button, an
   interval switch turned off, an interval switch turned on inside its open window, an Assist
   sentence, an LLM tool call): the event's context id and user are the caller's.
2. An automation on "Countdown started": its `automation_triggered` event has the call's context
   as its parent.
3. The device switched on by hand with a context: the start carries it. Switched off by hand:
   the end (`turned_off`) carries it.
4. Caused by time (expiry, limit, pending command and its start, window start and end, restart
   catch-up): no parent, no user, and an id unlike every earlier call's. Also 100 seconds after a
   button press.
5. "Add time" on an end that has passed: `expired` is fresh, the start carries the call.
6. Two calls with different contexts queued behind a slow call: each event has its own call's
   context.
7. A call that changes nothing fires nothing.

## Part B: entities

### #46 Two timers on one device are told apart by name

#### Why

On a device with two timers, such as a relay with two channels, both Run for entities are named
"Relay Run for", and so are their other entities. Only the entity id differs (`_2`). The target
picker and the dashboard list the same name twice.

#### The rule

- While a device holds more than one timer, each of its timers' entities names its target in
  brackets: "Relay Run for (Channel 1)", "Relay Ends at (Channel 2)", "Relay Schedule Morning
  (Channel 1)". That covers Run for, Ends at, Next command, Runtime, Turn off and the interval
  switches.
- A device with one timer keeps today's names.
- Adding a second timer to a device renames the first one at once. Deleting one of two makes the
  other plain again. The timer that is renamed is reloaded for it: its countdown, history and
  entity ids are kept, and its entities are unavailable for a moment, as at any change of its
  settings.
- Existing entities keep their entity ids. A new second timer gets ids after its new name, such
  as `number.relay_run_for_channel_2`, not `number.relay_run_for_2`.
- A name the user gave an entity is never changed.

#### Claude's calls

- **Only with more than one timer on the device.** Always naming the target would rename every
  installed entity, and "Boiler Run for (Boiler plug)" is noise on a device with one timer.
- **A second translation key per entity**, `<key>_with_target`, with the placeholder `{target}`
  in brackets: "Run for ({target})", "Schedule {interval} ({target})". The brackets need no
  inflection in Russian. Rejected: one key with an optional suffix (the translator loses the
  punctuation); overriding `name` (entity ids would follow the house language); child devices
  (the entities would leave the target's device page).
- **`{target}` is the target's name without its device's name**
  (`er.async_get_unprefixed_name`). When that is empty, or the target has no registry entry, it is
  the target's full name. One helper in `target.py`.
- **Every timer on the device counts**, loaded or not: a disabled or failed timer counts, and at
  start-up both timers see each other. The Voice settings entry has no target and never counts. A
  target with no device gets a device of its own per timer, so it is never named after its target.
- **One place reloads the siblings.** After a timer is set up, and when one is removed, the code
  in `__init__.py` compares each other timer on the device with what the count now says. It
  schedules a reload only for the ones whose naming differs, once per entry. A target that moves
  to another device already reloads its timer and runs the same check.
- **A renamed target does not reload the timer.** The bracket follows at the next load, like the
  entry's title.

#### Non-goals

- A name pattern the user can choose.
- Renaming entity ids of existing entities.

#### Scope

`entity.py` (the key and its placeholders), `target.py` (the name helper and the count of timers
on a device), `__init__.py` (the sibling reload), `translations/en.json` and `ru.json` (the six
`_with_target` keys). Tests that pin `_2` change (`test_automation_actions.py`,
`test_websocket.py`). The fake relay gets channel names of its own (`entity_name` on
`provide_fake`).

#### Docs

`docs/en/automations.md` and `docs/ru/automations.md`, "A device with two timers": rewrite the
paragraph that says both Run for entities have the same name. It now says each names its channel
in brackets, the ids of a timer added in 1.3.0 or later follow the name, and an older second
timer keeps `_2`. The advice to rename an entity goes, except as a way to choose a name of your
own. One commit.

#### Tests

1. One timer on a device: names and ids unchanged.
2. Two timers on one device after a restart: every entity of both reads differently.
3. A second timer added by the config flow while the first runs: the first is renamed by its
   reload, with the same ids. Its countdown ends on time, the device is not switched, and no
   event repeats.
4. One of two deleted: the other is plain again after its reload.
5. A name the user set survives 3 and 4.
6. A target with no device is never named after its target. A target that moves to the device of
   another timer: both are checked.
7. A target with an empty name of its own uses its full name. A disabled sibling still counts.

## Part C: voice

### #49 More ways of saying a clock time

#### Why

Commands at a clock time accept "at 7:30", "at 7 am" and parts of the day, but not "7:30am",
"half past 7" or "quarter to 8". Russian has the same kinds of forms: the half and the quarter
of the next hour, a quarter before the hour, and a part of the day said before the time. None of
them switches the device today: Assist says it did not understand.

#### The rule (owner's decisions)

One form covers the minutes around an hour. Any 1 to 59 minutes before or after an hour can be
said. "Quarter" is 15 and "half" is 30: they are only words for those numbers.

English:

- "N past H": "ten past 7", "half past 7", "quarter past 7", "twenty-five past 7".
- "N to H": "five to 8", "quarter to 8", "twenty minutes to 8", "quarter to noon",
  "quarter to midnight".
- "7:30am" and "7:30pm", with no space.
- "tonight at 7", "at 7 tonight", "this morning at 7", "this evening at half past 7".

Russian, all four groups:

- A day word, a part of the day and a time: "zavtra utrom v 7" (tomorrow morning at 7),
  "segodnya vecherom v 9" (this evening at 9). The adverbs are "utrom" (in the morning), "dnyom"
  (in the afternoon), "vecherom" (in the evening) and "noch'yu" (at night).
- Minutes of the next hour, with an ordinal hour: "v polovine vos'mogo" and "v polvos'mogo"
  (half past 7, literally "at the half of the eighth"), "v chetvert' vos'mogo" (quarter past 7),
  "desyat' minut vos'mogo" (ten past 7).
- Minutes before an hour: "bez chetverti vosem'" (quarter to 8, literally "eight without a
  quarter"), "bez pyati vosem'" (five to 8), "bez dvadtsati pyati vosem'" (twenty-five to 8).
- A time and a part of the day after it: "v 7:30 utra" (at 7:30 in the morning), "v 9 vechera"
  (at 9 in the evening). Both are understood already; this part pins them with tests.

The Russian ordinal hour: the Nth hour is the one that runs from N-1 to N. So "N minutes of the
eighth" is 7 and N minutes, and "of the first" (pervogo) is 12 and N minutes.

A "tonight", "this morning" or "segodnya vecherom" whose time has passed is refused and said,
as "today at 7" is today. It is not moved to tomorrow.

#### The constraint: no hardcoding

- Words live only in `custom_components/timerify/sentences/<lang>/src/`: the word lists (each
  word with its own number, such as half 30 and quarter 15) and the templates that put them in
  order. No list value holds a sum, a sign or a ready time.
- The direction is carried by the slot a template fills: `clock_minute` for minutes after the
  hour (as today), and the new `clock_before` (1 to 59) for minutes before it. The new
  `clock_ordinal` (1 to 12) names the Russian ordinal hour.
- One shared Python function does the arithmetic: `clock_moment` in `voice/commands.py` gains
  `before`, and the handler turns an ordinal into its hour (N-1, with 0 read as 12). Nothing in
  Python knows a word of any language.
- A third language needs only its files in `sentences/<lang>/src/`: its words, and a template
  line per form it has. Nothing in Python or in `scripts/` changes. The generators need no change;
  `core_commands.yaml` comes out byte-identical (checked by the research).

#### Claude's calls (the owner did not say)

- **The bare-hour rule holds for the new forms.** "half past 7" means the nearer of 07:30 and
  19:30 ahead, as "7:30" does. "quarter to 8" means the nearer of 07:45 and 19:45 ahead: said at
  19:50 it is tomorrow 07:45. "am", "pm", a part of the day and a leading zero apply to the named
  hour as they do today. (Superseded for a part of the day and for a bare 12 with a
  day word by "The hour 12 and a part of the day" below.)
- **Which hour a part of the day belongs to.** With "to" and with "bez", the named hour: "quarter
  to 8 in the evening" is 19:45, "quarter to midnight" is 23:45, "bez chetverti dvenadtsat'
  nochi" is 23:45. With the Russian ordinal, the hour counted from: "v polovine shestogo nochi"
  is 05:30 and "v polovine pervogo nochi" is 00:30. The subtraction is on the wall clock, after
  the day and the part are read, so the rules for midnight, night and daylight saving stay as
  they are. "tomorrow at quarter to midnight" is 23:45 tomorrow. (Superseded for a part of the day and for a bare 12 with a
  day word by "The hour 12 and a part of the day" below.)
- **"tonight" is today and night.** "tonight at 7" is 19:00 today. "tonight at 2" is 02:00 in
  the night after today, by the existing night rule. "this" is a day word that goes only before a
  part of the day.
- **Bounds.** 1 to 59 minutes, and hours 1 to 12 in the relative forms. In English "to noon"
  and "to midnight" are offered. "0 past 7", "60 to 8", "15 to 13", "half past noon" and "twenty
  to" with no hour are not understood. Russian "bez poloviny" is not offered.
- **"half to 8" is not understood.** English gets a list of words for minutes before the hour
  of its own, with "quarter" only, as Russian has "chetverti" only. So nonsense is not accepted
  as a side effect of one shared list.
- **Russian hour one after "bez" is the word "chas".** "bez pyati chas" is 12:55, by the
  bare-hour rule.
- **Left out, to add on request.** Digits as the ordinal ("v polovine 8"), "pol-vos'mogo" with a
  hyphen, "7:30a.m." glued with dots, and a bare "v chas". "pol vos'mogo" with a space is
  understood. "v" before an ordinal form is optional.
- **The ordinal hour names are a 12-word list in `src/`.** A test compares it with the number
  library's genitive ordinals (with yo folded to ye).
- **No new number words.** The existing range of minutes (1 to 59) and of hours (1 to 12) already
  matches digits and number words in every Russian case, so "bez dvadtsati pyati" needs no list
  of its own.
- **The gazetteer is left alone.** A bare "tonight" or "tomorrow morning" with no time after the
  device stays Home Assistant's (#21). These are not added.
- **LLM agents are unaffected.** The clock intent is sentence-only and is not offered as a tool.

#### Non-goals

- New languages.
- Seconds, or minutes said without an hour.
- Changing the existing rules for noon, midnight, night or a leading zero.

#### Scope

`sentences/en/src/timerify.yaml` and `sentences/ru/src/timerify.yaml` (lists and expansion
rules), the generated `sentences/<lang>/timerify.yaml` and `core_commands.yaml` (regenerated with
`uv run python -m scripts.generate_sentences` and `... generate_core_commands`), and
`voice/commands.py` (`DayPart` gains `tonight`, `clock_moment(..., before=)`, the handler's
slots). A new fixture `tests/fixtures/ru/clock_phrases.yaml`.

#### Docs

`docs/en/voice.md` and `docs/ru/voice.md`, "What you can say": add the new forms to the list of
clock times, with the bare-hour rule and the refusal of a past "tonight". "What you cannot say":
delete the bullet "Some ways of saying the time", and keep the heading. One commit. Also:
`RULINGS.md` (a sub-entry "Minutes around an hour" under the clock entry, and the entry "Parts of
the day in English words" rewritten), `docs/followups.md` (the line for #49 goes), and a comment
on #21 listing what is now understood.

#### Tests

From the user's side, with the real built-in agent, Jerusalem, as in
`tests/voice/test_clock_commands.py`.

1. English table: "7:30am", "7:30pm", "half past 7", "quarter past 7", "ten past 7", "quarter to
   8", "twenty to 8", "twenty-five to 8", "5 to 8", "ten minutes to eight", "quarter to 8 in the
   evening", "tonight at 7", "tonight at 2", "at 7 tonight", "this morning at 7", "this evening at
   half past 7", "tomorrow morning at quarter to 8", and the same with "turn off". Each gives its
   pending command at its time.
2. The same forms at different times of day (06:00, 08:00, 19:50, 20:00, 23:50), to pin the
   nearer-of rule with minutes before the hour.
3. Edges around 12: "quarter to 12" at 06:00 and at 12:00, "quarter to noon", "quarter to
   midnight", "today at quarter to midnight", "tomorrow at quarter to midnight", "ten to 1 at
   night".
4. "tonight at 7" said at 20:00, and "this morning at 7" said at 08:00: refused, nothing pending.

   vos'mogo vechera", "bez odnoy minuty vosem'", "bez pyati chas", "v polovine pervogo nochi",
   "bez chetverti dvenadtsat' nochi", "segodnya vecherom v 9" after 21:00 (refused). Negative:
   "dvadtsat' vos'mogo" (a date) and "bez dvadtsati" with no hour are not understood.
6. Not understood: "0 past 7", "60 to 8", "15 to 13", "half past noon", "half to 8", "twenty to".
   This replaces `test_the_clock_forms_not_understood_yet_are_not_understood`.

### The hour 12 and a part of the day (owner's decisions, 2026-11-04)

The final review of Part C found that a part of the day read on the named hour puts 12 twelve
hours away from what a speaker means: "tomorrow morning at quarter to 12" was 23:45,
"bez chetverti dvenadtsat' utra" 23:45, "v polovine dvenadtsatogo dnya" 23:30, and "this
evening at 12" noon. The owner decided two rules.

1. **A part of the day picks the result.** The time is first worked out on the dial, with the
   minutes past or before, which gives two candidates twelve hours apart. The part of the day
   then takes the one inside its hours. Each part has a window of twelve hours (the length is
   Claude's call, beyond the option the owner chose), so exactly one candidate is inside it and
   no new refusal is needed:
   - the morning and "am": 00:00 to 12:00 of the day;
   - the afternoon and the Russian "dnya"/"dnyom", and noon: 11:00 to 23:00;
   - the evening and the Russian "vechera"/"vecherom", and midnight: 13:00 to 01:00 of the next
     date;
   - the night, "tonight" and the Russian "nochi"/"noch'yu": 18:00 to 06:00 of the next date.
   With a day word the window is that of the named day ("tomorrow night" runs from 18:00
   tomorrow to 06:00 the day after). With "today" or "tonight" the window of the day before
   counts too, while its time is still ahead (the night in progress). Without a day word the
   next time ahead inside any window is taken. Results: "tomorrow morning at quarter to 12" is
   11:45, "v polovine dvenadtsatogo dnya" 11:30, "this evening at 12" and "this evening at half
   past 12" 00:00 and 00:30 after today, "v polovine pervogo nochi" 00:30. Every whole hour
   keeps its time, except "at 11 in the afternoon" or "v 11 dnya", now 11:00, and 12 in the
   evening, now midnight. The grammar keeps leaving 12 out of "tonight/this evening at 12 pm".
2. **A bare 12 with a day word is noon.** With a day word and no part of the day, the reading of
   12 after noon is taken when it is ahead, and the one after midnight only when it is not:
   "tomorrow at 12:30" and "zavtra v polovine pervogo" are 12:30 tomorrow, "today at quarter to
   12" is 11:45, or 23:45 once 11:45 has passed. Without a day word a bare 12 stays the nearer
   of its two times ahead.

## Part D: the card

### #53 An action given alone is the press

#### Why

Every Home Assistant card is configured with the action alone, such as `tap_action: {action:
toggle}`. On a Timerify card that does nothing today: the tap still opens the pop-up, because
`tap` keeps its default. The visual editor behaves the same way.

#### The rule (owner's decisions)

- A press with no choice of its own (`tap`, `double_tap`, `hold`) but with its `*_action` given
  does that action, as every Home Assistant card does.
- A press with a choice of its own does what the choice says, as today.
- In the visual editor, the press's select stays empty for such a press. Its helper text says
  that with no choice and an action given, the action runs. There is no custom editor.

#### Claude's calls

- **One resolver, in `parseConfig`.** The actions are parsed first. Each press is then resolved
  from three inputs: its choice, its action and its default. A choice wins. With no choice, a
  given action makes the press `action`. With neither, the default applies. Everything after
  `config.ts` reads the resolved press and does not change.
- **An action of "Nothing" makes the press nothing.** `{action: none}` resolves to `none`, with
  no choice and also with the choice `action`. So `hold_action: {action: none}` switches off the
  round tile's long press, and a double tap set to nothing never makes a tap wait 250 ms. As in
  Home Assistant, `tap_action: {action: none}` alone turns the tap off.
- **An action with no `action` key counts as not given.** It names nothing to do. The editor's
  "Default" writes no key at all.
- **What follows from the rule.** A `hold_action` alone gives the pill a long press. A
  `double_tap_action` alone makes a single tap wait for the double tap, as Home Assistant's cards
  do.
- **The helper texts.** The press selects get a helper: "Empty: runs the action below if one is
  set. Otherwise it does the default." The action helper becomes: "Used when the behavior above is
  "Home Assistant action" or empty." The Russian strings say the same.
- **The regression, said out loud.** A user who chose an action and later cleared the choice with
  the cross, to go back to the pop-up, keeps the action in the config. After 1.3.0 that press runs
  the action. The release notes and `card.md` say how to go back: set the action to "Default", or
  choose "Open the pop-up".

#### Non-goals

- A custom editor that shows an unset press as "Home Assistant action" (the form cannot show a
  value it does not hold; the research's option B).
- Changing a config in `assertConfig`.

#### Scope

`card/src/config.ts`, `card/src/editor.ts` (`computeHelper` for the presses, a comment),
`translations/en.json` and `ru.json`, the rebuilt bundle.

#### Docs

`docs/en/card.md` and `docs/ru/card.md`: "Add the card" (the YAML sample and the table), "The
visual editor" (an empty press), "Home Assistant actions" (an action alone is enough; how to go
back to the pop-up). One commit.

#### Tests

1. Config: an action alone gives `action`; a choice wins; `{action: none}` gives `none`, with no
   choice and with `action`; an action replaces the round tile's default long press; an action
   with no `action` key leaves the default; an invalid action is still an error.
2. Presses: only `tap_action: toggle` sends the action for the tap and opens no pop-up; only
   `double_tap_action` makes the tap wait; only `hold_action` gives the pill a long press.
3. Editor: the press helper and the action helper show, in both languages.
4. E2E: set only "Tap action" to "Toggle" in the visual editor. The saved config has
   `tap_action` and no `tap`, and a tap on the card toggles the device.

### #52 The round tile's dial fits the tile

#### Why

The dial is 80 px wide. A round tile at its smallest size, 2 columns, is narrower on every
screen, and 3 columns is narrower on a 320 px phone. The dial is cut by up to 18 px.

#### The rule (owner's decision)

The dial shrinks with its tile. The tile's minimum size stays 2 columns. The dial, its ring, its
time and its icon stay whole and readable at every size the tile allows. Where the tile has room,
the dial is 80 px, as today.

**Amended (owner's decision, 2026-11-04).** In the real Home Assistant a 2-column round tile on
a 320 px phone is 44 px wide, so the dial is 26 px and its time 4 to 7 px: whole, but not
readable. Where the dial is too small for a readable time (below about 45 px, measured in the
E2E), it shows the progress ring and the icon instead of the time, as Home Assistant's own small
tiles do. The remaining time is still in the pop-up and in the text a screen reader reads. A
browser without container queries keeps the 80 px dial with its time.

#### Claude's calls

- **The dial is its own container.** `.dial` gets `width: 80px; max-width: 100%; aspect-ratio:
  1; container-type: inline-size` and no fixed height. The ring is an SVG with a view box, so it
  already scales. Inside, the time is `min(<today's size>, 20cqw)` and the icon `min(32px,
  40cqw)`, with the same `min()` for the Mushroom style. The 20 and 40 are first values, measured
  in the E2E.
- **Not the tile's content.** Making `.round .content` a container (the issue's wording) would
  take away the rule that a card without the Turn off button is laid out as before, including one
  sized by its content. The dial has an explicit width, so its containment costs nothing there.
  `round.spec.ts` keeps pinning that `.content` is no container without the button.
- **The browser floor.** Container queries and `cqw` need Chrome or Edge 105, Safari 16 or
  Firefox 110. The card is built for es2022 and Home Assistant loads it as a module, so a browser
  that runs the card at all but lacks `cqw` is one of roughly Chrome and Edge 94 to 104, Firefox
  94 to 109 and Safari 15. Such a browser ignores the whole container block and keeps the 80 px
  dial of today, with its time.

#### Non-goals

- A larger minimum size of the tile.
- Changing the Turn off button's slot or the card's height.

#### Scope

`card/src/round.ts`, the rebuilt bundle, `scripts/e2e/hass.py` (a new view),
`card/e2e/sizes.spec.ts`.

#### Docs

None for users: the screenshots show wider tiles and do not change. `RULINGS.md` (the note that
the dial is known to be wider is rewritten) and `docs/followups.md` (the line for #52 goes).

#### Tests

1. A new E2E view: a round tile without the button at its minimum (2 columns) and at 3 columns,
   with a countdown over an hour running (the longest text, `H:MM:SS`). At 1280, 390 and 320 px
   the dial lies inside the card and its time is not cut. The Mushroom sizes view carries the same
   check.
2. The existing round tile with the button at 3 columns on a 320 px phone: the comment that the
   dial is wider becomes an assertion that it is inside the card.
3. `round.spec.ts`: `.content` is still no container without the button, and the dial is 80 px
   where there is room.

### #51 The pop-up says why it shows no presets

#### Why

The pop-up hides a preset longer than the timer's "Maximum of Run for". When every preset is

When every preset is longer than the maximum, a line stands where the row would be: "Every preset

- **One new card string**, `card_presets_above_maximum`, with the blank `{maximum}`, in `en.json`
  and `ru.json`. It uses the existing `.note` style of the pop-up.

  and the filter by it, so the line can name the maximum.

#### Non-goals

#### Scope

`card/src/popup.ts`, `translations/en.json` and `ru.json`, the rebuilt bundle.

### #63 A weekday in a label that does not tick is right after midnight

#### Why

The card names the weekday of a time that is not today, such as "by Tue 00:30 at the latest" for
a limit, or the time of a pending command on an idle timer. Neither ticks. After midnight the
label still says "Tue", although the time is now today, until something else redraws the card.

#### The rule

At the first midnight after which such a label is wrong, the card and its open pop-up redraw
once. "by Tue 00:30 at the latest" becomes "by 00:30 at the latest". A date label (such as "Nov
9") becomes a weekday on the day it should. The card does not tick for this: it redraws once per
midnight, and only while a label with a day is shown.

#### Claude's calls

- **A midnight controller.** A second Lit `ReactiveController`, next to the countdown's, with
  one timeout to the next midnight. It is armed only while a moment shown is on another day. After
  each redraw it checks again: a new moment, a new zone or a removed card rearms or clears it.
- **The zone is the one the label is written in.** That is Home Assistant's time zone, or the
  browser's when the user's profile asks for local time. The rule is taken out of `formatClock`
  into one function, so the label and the controller always agree.
- **The clock is the server's.** "Now" is the browser's clock corrected by the offset measured at
  lookup, as the countdown does. The delay is a difference of two moments on that timeline, so the
  offset cancels out.
- **Exact on the days the clocks change.** The next midnight is found as the first moment whose
  calendar day in the zone is later, by bisection over at most 49 hours, to one second. A plain
  "24 hours minus the time since midnight" is an hour wrong on those days. `Temporal` is not used:
  not every supported browser has it.
- **Two instances.** The card's covers the limit's label of the pill and the round tile's status.
  The pop-up's, while it is open, covers the limit and every pending command it lists, also on an
  idle timer.
- **After the device sleeps.** A timeout can fire late after a tablet sleeps. When the page
  becomes visible again, the controller redraws once if a label with a day is shown, and rearms.

#### Non-goals

- A label of a time today, which ends with a state change that redraws the card anyway.
- Ticking every second.

#### Scope

`card/src/format.ts` (the zone function and the next midnight), a new `card/src/midnight.ts`,
`timerify-card.ts` and `popup.ts` (wiring), the rebuilt bundle.

#### Docs

None for users. `RULINGS.md` (the sentence on the cost in "A limit does not tick" is rewritten)
and `docs/followups.md` (the line for #63 goes).

#### Tests

Vitest with fake timers, and the fake Home Assistant's time zone and profile zone.

1. A limit "by Tue 00:30" at Monday 23:59:30: 31 seconds later the label reads "00:30", with no
   state change.
2. The same for a pending command of an idle timer in the open pop-up.
3. A date label turns into a weekday at midnight six days before.
4. Profile zone "server" against "local" with different zones: the redraw follows the zone of the
   label.
5. The next midnight on the days the clocks change in Europe/London (last Sunday of March and of
   October) is exact.
6. No timeout while nothing shown has a day, and none after the card is removed.
7. A page that becomes visible after midnight redraws the label.
